# Players install the seventies; Street Corsa links them

About **25 to 30 usable AC1 street-car mods** could take Street Corsa's garage from 1972 to 1979, but almost none of them can ship with an open-source game. The 1970-72 muscle cars are well covered, and the installed set already has most of them. From 1973 to 1979 the pool shrinks to a handful of cars: the **1977 Trans Am, 1979 Camaro Z28, 1978 Mustang II King Cobra, 1972 and 1974 Gran Torinos, the 1980 "Bell's" Corvette C3, Gremlin X, Pacer X, 1976 Jeep CJ5, 1978 Checker Marathon, 1972 C-10 and 1979 Chevy LUV**. Most of these come from the Smallblockhero (SBH) and Uncle M family, which converts models from Forza, CSR Classics and other games. So they work as cars a player installs, but they cannot be redistributed. Whole classes have **no AC1 mod at all**: 1970s land yachts (Cadillac, Lincoln, LTD, Caprice), 1970s Ford and Dodge pickups, the Bronco, Blazer, Pinto, Chevette, Duster, 1973-79 Mopars, the 1970-72 GTO and the 1970s Cutlass. The imports are in better shape. Four free original models stand out: Alguecool's Datsun Fairlady 2000, which is explicitly free, unencrypted and shareable with credit, Pessio's Mini Cooper S, |Viper|'s Giulietta Spider and Stereo's 1976 CJ5. These come from the authors worth asking for permission. The one strong new release of 2025-26 is MJC Solutions' free Valiant Charger trilogy (1972-76, Australian Mopar). The practical model is the one AssettoServer and Content Manager use: ship a manifest with links to each author's resource page, and let players install. A caveat applies to the whole report. Nothing was downloaded, and assettoworld, assettocorsamods.io, ModLand and Patreon blocked fetching. So **encryption is unknown for nearly every car**, and many details below rest on search snippets.

## Twelve installs push the garage to 1979

The ranking below favours cars that add a body or year the installed set lacks, are free, are not known to be encrypted and run close to stock specs. Street Corsa rebuilds the engine curve and scales the running gear from its own part tree, so a mod's fantasy physics matter less than its body and model. A "tuned" mod with 700 bhp is still a usable shell. Provenance decides only whether the car can ever ship. Every pick needs a manual check of its KN5 header for encryption before it goes into `cars.json`.

## Ship nothing third-party today, but six authors are worth asking

No car mod found in any group carries a formal open licence (no CC-BY, CC0 or MIT). The default is all rights reserved. Overtake's terms grant the licence to Overtake only, and they forbid "deep-linking to our resources and modding files for any purpose without explicit authorization" ([Overtake terms](https://www.overtake.gg/help/terms/)). The closest open-source precedent is AssettoServer. Its documentation tells server owners to "use the download links the authors of the content you're using provide unless you're explicitly allowed to reupload them", and Content Manager's `cm_content/content.json` points players at the missing cars ([AssettoServer FAQ](https://github.com/compujuckel/assettoserver-docs/blob/master/docs/faq.md)). Street Corsa should follow that pattern. Each manifest entry would hold the folder id, author, **resource-page URL** (never a direct file link and never an aggregator), version and a free/paid/encrypted flag. The game shows "get it" links for missing cars and works with whatever the player has installed. Two groups can be shipped: content Street Corsa owns, and content whose author has given written permission, recorded in a permissions file. Kunos cars such as the Miura, 33 Stradale and Abarth 595 SS are in every player's install anyway, so they cost nothing to support and must never be copied.

Most of the catalogue can never move into that shippable group, whatever its author says. The AC Legends and SBH credits name the model sources as "Turn10 Forza, SimBin, Retro Expansion, Grid" ([DrivingItalia](https://www.drivingitalia.net/forums/topic/78229-ac-legends-mod-gt-legends-in-assetto-corsa/)). The TransAm Legends notes warn that "due to various sources the model quality may vary per car" ([release notes](https://thracing.de/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/2020-03-26-AC-TransAm-Legends-v2.0-Release-Notes-1.pdf)). Users describe GeroDa74's payware as "ripped models (from Forza, Need For Speed etc) using physics ripped from Kunos cars" ([Overtake thread](https://www.overtake.gg/threads/how-to-get-geroda74s-mods.204958/)). For a ripped car, the permission is not the modder's to give. The same holds for re-host packs: GrippedUp's Wild Stallions pack calls itself a "community preservation pack" in which "original credit remains with their original creators" ([GrippedUp](https://grippedupmods.com/downloads/car-packs/wild-stallions-muscle-car-pack)). One installed car, `wild_stallions_pontiac_gto65_restomod`, comes from that pack, so it should be flagged as non-shippable as well.

The authors worth contacting all make original models and publish under their own name on Overtake. **Alguecool & Pasta2000** (Fairlady 2000) already allow sharing with credit, so a short confirmation would make that car the first shippable one, subject to the general Datsun trademark question. **Stereo** made the 1976 CJ5 and the "1950s American Traffic – Big 3" set: a 1956 Bel Air, a 1957 Mercury Commuter wagon and a 1959 Dodge Coronet sedan. These are original models with AI-traffic-grade physics ([Overtake](https://www.overtake.gg/downloads/1950s-american-traffic-big-3.51906/)). **MJC Solutions** says "My mods will always be free". **Pessio** has a record of granting model permission, since AC Legends used his Pantera Gr.4 with it ([DrivingItalia](https://www.drivingitalia.net/forums/topic/78229-ac-legends-mod-gt-legends-in-assetto-corsa/)). **Joshua Widder** made the 1970 AMC Javelins himself: "3D Modeller, Physics, Textures – Joshua Widder" ([Overtake](https://www.overtake.gg/downloads/1970-amc-javelins.46012/)). He also made the highly rated "1970's Performance Mustangs", whose model origin his page does not state. **|Viper|** (with Anton Gruzdev for physics) made the Giulietta Spider. Bazza's THR Touring Car Legends packs ([THR](https://thracing.de/touring-car-legends/)) are tempting because their physics come from real data and they cover many US-sold imports. They are a weaker ask, though: some cars sit on Kunos DLC models and others on SBH conversions. Not worth asking: SBH, Uncle M, GeroDa74, AC Legends and Donnie Heerdt (rip lineage), MacedoSTI and EX-MODS (encrypted, plus a bought Hum3D shell for the C-10), and every anonymous re-host.

## No mod exists for most of the 1970s showroom

The empty space shapes the roadmap more than the catalogue does. No AC1 mod was found for any of these: the **1970s Cadillac, Lincoln, Buick, Oldsmobile, Chrysler or Imperial** (no Eldorado, Mark IV/V, Electra, Delta 88, Toronado, New Yorker or Cordoba); the **Ford LTD, 1970s Thunderbird, Mercury Marquis, Cyclone, 1971-73 Cougar, Torino Cobra/Talladega or Pinto**; the **1970s Impala, Caprice, Nova, 1971-77 Chevelle, Chevette, or a 1973-79 street Corvette** apart from the 1980 Bell's; the **1970-74 GTO, Grand Prix, LeMans, 1970s Cutlass/442 or Buick GS/Regal**; the **1972-74 Barracuda, Duster, Dart, Aspen, Volaré, 1970s Fury or 1973-79 Charger and Challenger**; and the **Matador, Hornet SC/360 or 1971-74 Javelin AMX** as street cars. There is also nothing in these classes: 1970s wagons, Ford, Dodge or square-body Chevy pickups after the C-10, the Li'l Red Express, Bronco, Blazer, Ranchero, 1971-79 El Camino, or Ford and Dodge vans. On the import side there is no Hilux, FJ40, Honda N600 or stock Civic, Mazda REPU, RX-7 SA22, B210, 260Z/280Z, Rabbit or Scirocco (the ones listed are Automobilista or GT Legends mods), 912, street 924 or 1978-79 928, BMW E9/E21/1600 street car, W114/W115, R107 or W123, Opel GT or Kadett B, Capri Mk2, Volvo 140, GT6, TR7, XJ6, Fiat 124 Spider, Alfa Duetto or GTV 105.

For the 1950s and 1960s the gaps are just as large: the Studebaker Avanti, Hawk and Lark, Rocket 88, Toronado, Skylark, Wildcat, Chrysler 300 letter cars, DeSoto, Edsel, Nash, Kaiser, Willys, postwar Packard, AMX, Rambler, US Falcon, Ranchero, 1968-69 Torino, 1960s F-100, Apache, Dodge and International trucks, 1959-69 Fury, Polara, 1964-67 Barracuda, 1955-57 Thunderbird and 1950s-60s Cadillac. Absence from search does not prove a car is absent, because Patreon-only and Discord-only releases are poorly indexed. Still, it does show that the Street Corsa car lot will lean on muscle cars unless the project models its own everyday cars. A good first commission would be a 1970s full-size sedan, a pickup and a wagon. Those bodies would also become shippable base content.

## Verify these by hand before trusting them

Some hits belong to **other sims**, not AC1. Steam Workshop items such as the "1971 & 72 Road Runner", the "Oldsmobile Delta 88" and the David_Import "1959 Cadillac Eldorado" (AC1 has no Workshop) are among them. So are the Brickyard Legends Camaro II and Greenwood Corvette, which install into a GT Legends folder structure ([Overtake](https://www.overtake.gg/downloads/chevrolet-camaro-ii-imsa-transam-etcc-by-brickyard-legends-team.21998/)), the esport-racing.de "Corvette 1974 RE" and "Pinto 1971" (rFactor/GTR2), ClimaxF1's Golf Mk1 (Automobilista), the Customs Compagneros Scirocco and Butch's P1800 and 300SL (GT Legends), and DAZZERX's BMW 2002 tii (rFactor 2). The aggregator year tags are unreliable as well. The "Cadillac DTS Presidential Limo" is tagged as a 1972 car, but it is a 2006+ model ([assettocorsamods.io](https://assettocorsamods.io/cars/muscle+car/cadillac_dts_presidental_limo/)). The ModLand "1970's Performance Mustangs" listing is probably Joshua Widder's Overtake release under another name.

Still open: the **encryption status of every row marked "?"** (fix by downloading and checking the KN5 headers); the author and origin behind every † row; the price and terms of every Patreon item; the licence clauses in the readme files and credits PDFs of the THR, AC Legends, Pessio and |Viper| downloads; and the exact wording of Overtake's clause on "encrypted files". A cheap local step remains too. Read the `ui_car.json` author and URL fields of the 167 installed folders to build the same provenance record for what the game already runs.

## Conclusion

The pool of 1970s cars is too small, and its provenance too poor, to be the content strategy on its own. What the AC1 community offers for 1973-79 is a dozen mostly ripped shells and nothing for the everyday cars that made up a real 1970s street. Street Corsa's design helps here. The game re-derives engines and running gear from its own part tree, so a ripped shell with fantasy physics still works as a player-installed car, and a manifest with resource-page links keeps the repo clean. The things that cannot be fixed later are model ownership and encryption.

That points to a two-track plan. Players get a recommended-install list now, led by the Trans Am, Camaro '79, King Cobra, Gran Torino, CJ5 and Checker. In parallel, the project asks the few original-model authors (Alguecool, Stereo, MJC, Pessio, Widder, |Viper|) for permission to ship and commissions the missing everyday bodies. Those bodies are the only cars an open-source Street Corsa will ever fully own.
